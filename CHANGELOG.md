# Changelog

All notable changes to Blip. Semantic versioning; `-wip` marks pre-releases where many features may be broken.

## [0.2.2-wip] — 2026-11-14 (fix: left/right mirrored; frozen-target filter)
- **Fixed: left and right were swapped for both radars.** Blip assumed +x = the radar's left; the RD-03D and the LD2450 both report +x = right (ESPHome's rd03d and ld2450 docs; Hi-Link's LD2450 manual). So stepping right moved the dot left, which with the modules' coarse angle (2°–20°) looked random. Fixed on the phone scope and the OLED. Reported by the author on both modules: "moving closer and further works but left/right is quite random". *Mirror L/R* is still there for a radar mounted upside down. Not yet re-checked on hardware.
- **New: drop frozen targets** (Settings, default **10 s**, off/5/10/30/60): a detection reported at exactly the same x and y, to the millimetre, for longer than that is dropped, and comes back the moment it moves. The LD2450 is known to fixate on a phantom (fan, glass, reflection) at byte-identical coordinates after it has gone; author reported "if it sees a ghost it locks the ghost in memory even once it's gone". *Show filtered* labels these "stuck". In `gate.h` / `gate_py.py`; tests: phantom dropped after 10 s and back after a 1 mm move, a still person who wobbles a few mm never dropped, and a phantom that misses frames among moving targets still dropped (that case found and fixed a slot-eviction bug while writing the test).
- Checked in the code: the coordinate decoding itself (sign bit set = positive) matches Hi-Link's spec and ESPHome; the tracker drops lost tracks after 1 s and the page only draws the latest frame, so the lingering ghost came from the module, not from Blip's memory.

## [0.2.2-wip] — 2026-11-13 (fix: S3 boot loop on 4 MB boards; 2 MB flash layout)
- **Fixed:** the S3 builds were 16 MB images (the Waveshare S3-Pico's size). On an S3 board with 4 MB flash the chip refused to start and rebooted forever ("Detected size(4096k) smaller than the size in the binary image header(16384k)"), so there was no Wi-Fi. Found by the author on an S3 + HLK-LD2450 build.
- **All six builds now use one 2 MB layout** (`firmware/partitions_2mb.csv`: NVS, PHY, a 1.875 MB app, core dump; no OTA), DIO flash mode. They boot on any ESP32-C3/S3 with 2 MB flash or more; larger chips leave the rest unused. The app is 1.14 MB (S3) to 1.27 MB (C3), about 60% of its slot. NVS stays at 0x9000, so a C3 keeps its settings; an S3-Pico that ran the old 16 MB layout starts with default settings once.
- S3 builds also copy the log to the chip's own USB port, so S3 boards without a CH343 (USB socket straight to the S3) show it in the flasher's USB log too. Not yet checked on hardware.
- Still to confirm on the author's board: that it now boots and the hotspot appears; and, if the board is not a Waveshare S3-Pico, whether its pins match (radar on GPIO11/12, OLED on 17/18).

## [0.2.2-wip] — 2026-11-08 (static, post-bump dropout, pattern recognition)
Everything below is verified on a PC with synthetic signals only. **Nothing here has been heard on real hardware yet**; see `docs/STETHO_SIGNAL_NOTES.md` for the A/B procedure.
- **Fix: half a second of lag and heavy attenuation after a bump.** The old chain muted by 30 dB for 150 ms plus a 120 ms release, and the AGC and noise-floor trackers kept learning from the knock, so the gain stayed 15 to 48 dB too low for seconds afterwards. Now: a shallow duck (5 to 18 dB, scaled by the Bump rejection slider, 60 ms hold, 25 ms release); a 2 ms look-ahead peak limiter so a knock is limited and not muted; the AGC, level and noise-floor estimators freeze during a bump or a detected movement (300 ms tail, 3 s cap); and a level-excursion detector that holds the AGC and speeds up the level decay even when neither detector fires. Tests: signal back within 4 to 9 dB one beat after a knock and within 2 dB four beats after; a knock never exceeds the limiter ceiling.
- **Burst blanker (Heart and Lungs presets).** A noise-blanker for short loud bursts above the preset's passband (the "5 to 10 heavy breaths a second" static fits the Wi-Fi beacon every 102.4 ms, which is a hypothesis, not a measurement). It watches the band above the passband, and when a burst far above its slow reference appears it gates the look-ahead delay path for at most 12 ms, then waits 150 ms before it can fire again. Off for Voice, Wide and Raw. Switch: Stetho tab, Experiments, Burst blanker.
- **Averaged ADC sampling (experiment).** 2, 4 or 8 readings per output sample with the extremes dropped, to remove ADC spikes before they are heard. Default stays at 1 until it has been compared on hardware. Stetho tab, Experiments, ADC sampling (or `/api/lab?os=4`).
- **Heart and breath pattern recognition in the page** (`CycleTracker`): autocorrelation to find the repeat period (walking down from multiples so 72 bpm is not read as 36), then folding the signal on that period for a fine rate, a beat template and a lock quality. Rates show `?` when the rhythm is only roughly steady and nothing when there is no pattern. Replaces the zero-crossing breath estimate and the first-autocorrelation-peak heart estimate. Synthetic results: 48 to 140 bpm within 2.5 bpm, noise-only and irregular knocks never reach a full lock, breath rate within 1 per minute from chest motion where the old estimate was off by 2 to 28.
- **Beat lock (optional, off by default):** once a heart or breath rhythm is locked, the sound between beats is turned down by 9 dB (heart) or 6 dB (lungs). Test: the heart sounds change by less than 1 dB and the gaps drop 5 to 7 dB.
- **Record 10 s to WAV** button (8 kHz, 16-bit, named with preset, averaging and blanker settings) and **`tools/analyze_wav.py`**, which reports clipping, band energy, whether short bursts repeat on the Wi-Fi beacon interval, and the heart or breath rhythm. This is how to find out what the static really is.
- Tests added: `tests/test_dsp_dynamics.cpp` (sanitizers, in CI), `tests/test_vitals_pr.js` (Node, in CI), `tests/test_analyze_wav.py`. The RV32 cost check with the new chain: about 7.7% of the C3 core (11.6% at 1.5 cycles per instruction).

## [0.2.2-wip] — 2026-11-07
- **Stetho stability test lab (no board needed for most of it).** `tools/soak_test.py` is a stdlib-only soak/bisect harness: it opens the same WebSocket the app does, counts audio packets and gaps, watches the node's boot counter for resets, and flips the new runtime **Lab** switches (`/api/lab`: pacing of `loop()`, DSP bypass, send policy, synthetic audio source instead of the ADC, text pushes, quiet logging, skipped subsystems, audio task priority) so ONE flash can isolate the freeze by trial instead of one reflash per theory. `tests/test_soak.py` tests the harness against `tools/sim_radar.py` (which now emulates the lab, 503-while-down and a crash). `tests/test_dsp_stability.cpp` runs the DSP chain through hostile scenes under UBSan/ASan. `tests/rv32_cost/run.py` compiles the audio path for RV32IMC, runs it in an emulator and counts instructions: Stetho costs about 7% of the C3 core (10% at 1.5 cycles/instruction), so plain CPU starvation by the DSP is ruled out. See `docs/STETHO_STABILITY.md`.
- **Fix: the bump gate could stay latched.** A sustained loud scene held the gate's slow-envelope tracker frozen indefinitely (a measured 10 s before release). The freeze is now capped at 600 ms; release is within 750 ms. Test added.
- **Fix: status JSON length arithmetic** could underflow on a long string; appends are now clamped (`jcat`) in a 2 KB buffer.
- The async_tcp freeze itself is **not** claimed fixed; the Lab build is how to find it.

- **Fix: stopping Stetho crashed the node.** A bench-test log decoded against its exact build showed `assert failed: xTaskPriorityDisinherit` from `adc_lock_release` from `adc_continuous_stop` from `Stetho::adcEnd()`. The ADC driver holds a mutex for as long as it samples, and FreeRTOS asserts if a different task gives it back: the ADC was opened by the web/loop task and closed by the audio task, so every stop (Stetho switched off, auto-sleep, a failed start) reset the board. The audio task now opens and closes the ADC itself, `start()` waits for it to report whether it came up, and its stack is 8 KB. This is not the `async_tcp` crash, but it is a real bug on the same feature.
- **Bench test stage 2 now streams to a real client.** The first bench log ran the full 2 minutes, twice, with Stetho sampling and a phone joined to the hotspot and no crash, but a bare Wi-Fi join opens no WebSocket, so no Stetho audio was sent at all. Stage 2 now serves a small page at `http://192.168.4.1/` that opens a WebSocket and counts the audio, the timer starts when that page connects, and each second's line shows pages open and packets sent/skipped. That is the load the app has when it crashes. The PASS line is printed before Stetho is stopped.
- **A/B test: the old Stetho send logic is back.** Stetho stopped working completely after the latency rework. The rework called `ws.binaryAll()` on every audio and vitals packet and let a smaller per-phone queue (8 instead of the library's 32) refuse them. That kept `loopTask` inside AsyncWebSocket's client and queue locks, and lwIP's `tcp_write`, dozens of times a second: the same locks `async_tcp` needs, and where the decoded crash found `loopTask`. This build puts back the send logic that ran for minutes on a real C3: send only when every phone has room (`availableForWriteAll`), only the newest two packets per pass, throw away a backlog of six (~190 ms). The queue cap is back to the library default (32). Mu-law 32 ms packets are kept. If Stetho runs again, the send path was the cause.
- **Bench test in the flasher:** a separate **🩺 Diagnostics** tab (next to *⚡ Flash Blip*) flashes the bundled bench image for the chosen board (envs renamed `c3-supermini-bench` / `s3-pico-bench`, images in `firmware/prebuilt/`, built by CI). New stage-1 checks: **fake flash** (read-only address wrap-around test and the chip's own capacity ID) and a **PSRAM** size and address-pattern walk on the S3-Pico. The RAM march now tests on-chip SRAM only. Stage 2 sends exactly as the app now does.
- **Standalone bench test** (`firmware/bench/`, envs `bench-c3` / `bench-s3`): a separate firmware, not the Blip app, for checking a board before you wire it up — in case a bad batch is part of the crash above. Stage 1 runs ~15 s of self-checks with no wiring needed (chip identity, a RAM march test, a flash/NVS scratch write, a Wi-Fi scan + AP bring-up, a Stetho-pin ADC read, an I2C scan, a UART loopback that's skipped rather than failed if nothing's jumpered). Stage 2 then runs the real Stetho sampler against the real Wi-Fi AP + web server for 2 minutes with a phone or laptop joined — the exact combination that has been crashing some boards — and if the board resets instead of finishing, the next boot reports it as failed and how far it got, using an RTC marker that survives the reset. See `firmware/bench/README.md`.
- **The disconnect-burst fix did not stop the crash.** A fresh node log showed the same `async_tcp` watchdog reset with no Wi-Fi client ever leaving or rejoining nearby — it just needs Stetho running and a client present, 4-9 seconds later. That rules out the previous theory. The failed-allocation hook never fired either, which rules out running out of memory. What's left points at CPU time, not memory: on this single-core chip, Stetho's sampling task and the Wi-Fi/networking stack are competing for the same core, and something about it is starving the networking task for several seconds straight. The health line now runs every 1 second (not 10) while Stetho is on, since the last two crashes happened faster than the old interval could ever catch twice, and any single `loop()` pass over 150 ms is now logged the instant it happens rather than waiting for the next health tick. This build does not claim to fix the crash — it's aimed at finally catching it in the act.
- **Found the real crash, from a user's node log (superseded above):** a C3 froze and watchdog-reset a few seconds after Stetho started, next to a client joining/leaving the hotspot. Decoding the crash addresses against the exact build showed `loopTask` inside `AsyncWebSocket`'s own internal client-list lock — the same lock the library takes while handling a client disconnect. Stetho's drain loop (`while` over every queued audio packet) could fire a burst of `ws.binaryAll()` calls back to back right at that moment. The drain is now capped at 4 packets per `loop()` pass, spreading any backlog over a few ticks instead of bursting it. Also added: a hook that logs the exact size and caller of the *first* failed heap allocation (so a crash has a concrete cause, not just a watchdog panic), and `async_tcp`'s own state (Running/Ready/Blocked) and stack headroom in the health line, so a future crash log shows what the networking task was doing, not just that it stopped checking in.
- **Node log** (like Skipper Flipper's): timestamped lines kept in 4 KB of RTC memory, which survives a crash or restart (not a power cut). The log has the start reason, Wi-Fi join/leave with reason codes, pages connecting and disconnecting, Stetho start/stop, and a **health line every 10 s**: free memory (lowest, biggest block), devices on the hotspot, open pages, requests and captive-portal probes, loop time and the Stetho counts. See it under Settings → *Node log* (Refresh / Save as file), at `/api/log`, or over USB with the flasher's new **🔌 USB log** button (no flashing). This is to find the memory drain seen on a C3 (139 KB → 23 KB free within a couple of minutes, then the node stops answering).
- **Wi-Fi transmit power setting** (8.5 / 15 / 19.5 dBm). It defaults to 8.5 dBm on the ESP32-C3 Super Mini, whose weak antenna makes Android phones struggle to join at full power, and 15 dBm on S3 boards.
- The WIP banner shows the **build time**, so you can tell which build is running even when the version number is unchanged.
- **Fix: the node froze a few seconds after Stetho started** (web server dead, no restart), seen on a real C3. The separate WebSocket sender task from the earlier 0.2.2 test builds is gone, and all sending is back in `loop()`, which ran for minutes in the earlier test. Kept: mu-law 32 ms packets, the 8-message queue cap per phone and the 380 ms node buffer. The page says *Stetho is starting…* instead of a false *could not start* in the moment after it is switched on.
- **Crash/stall diagnostics:** the node reports why it last restarted (power on, crash, watchdog, brown-out) and how many times it has started; the Stetho box shows it with the uptime, and says "No update from the node for N s" with the page's reconnect count when updates stop. The serial monitor prints the same at start-up.
- **Stetho audio sent as mu-law in 32 ms packets** (`'U'` packets, 256 bytes): half the bytes and a quarter of the messages of the old 16 ms int16 packets. The status is pushed every 2 s, and the page falls back to asking over HTTP if pushes stop. "No reading yet" shows as *measuring…* instead of "−0.00 V". The page still plays the old `'A'` packets.
- **Firmware build log** `firmware/prebuilt/BUILD_LOG.md`: `make_prebuilt.py --note` records each bundled build (version, time, source hash, SHA-256 per image). Only the last 3 builds' binaries are kept in git history, to keep the repo small.
- **The launcher opens the Blip Flasher first:** a red "maroon radar" page at `/` that is only the flasher, with a small *Radar simulator ›* button. The simulator moved to `/sim` and has a *⚡ Flasher* button back. The Flash tab inside the simulator is gone, to remove confusion. Phones on `--lan` and other hosts get the simulator, never the flasher.
- **Fix: Stetho audio lag (seconds) and the board's web server dying after a few minutes.** Seen on a C3: 84 packets sent, 7,567 dropped. Stetho packets now go out from their own task the moment they are made, instead of waiting for `loop()`. Each phone's WebSocket queue is capped at 8 messages (`WS_MAX_QUEUED_MESSAGES`), so a slow link skips audio instead of building up delay or memory. The node's status is pushed over the WebSocket once a second, instead of the page opening a new HTTP connection every second. Targets are only sent when a new radar frame arrives (1 Hz when there is no radar). The OLED is probed at start and switched off if nothing answers, so a missing display can't stall the node. USB serial output never waits for a PC that isn't reading. The Stetho tab shows free memory, the slowest loop and uptime, so a leak would show. Not yet re-tested on hardware.
- **Stetho diagnostics:** the Stetho tab now says why nothing is showing: Stetho off (with a *Turn it on* button), asleep, the ADC failed to start (with the reason), no samples yet, or the live voltage on the ADC pin with hints for "stuck near 0 V", "stuck at the top (MAX4466 on 5 V?)" and "flat, no signal". It refreshes every second while the tab is open. The serial monitor prints a Stetho health line every 5 s. Status JSON gains `stetho.err/samples/invalid/sent/level`. The audio task's stack is raised to 6 KB and a failed task start is reported.
- Fix: starting the simulator while an old one was still running crashed with "Address already in use". It now stops the old Blip simulator and takes over (or uses the next free port if another program holds it), and prints this computer's network address. `--lan` also listens on the network so a phone can open it.
- **HLK-LD2450 option** (`-DRADAR_LD2450=1`, envs `c3-supermini-ld2450` / `s3-pico-ld2450`): same 30-byte frame, so the same parser; no RD-03D mode commands are sent; Single mode is picked in firmware (`rd03d::keep_nearest`). Status reports `model`. Native tests added. Not yet tested on hardware.
- Fix: a `volatile` decrement warning in the Stetho mute counter.
- **Flash tab** in the simulator page replaces the three `Flash-*` scripts: pick board, radar and port, press one button; five-step progress, upload percentage, colour-coded log with *Copy*, plain-English tips for the usual failures, serial output afterwards, *Cancel*. Installs PlatformIO into `.venv` on the first run. Only answers this computer (loopback + Host check + per-run token); `--no-flash` hides it. `tools/flasher.py`, `tools/serial_tail.py`, `web/flasher.html`; tested with `tests/test_flasher.py` + `tests/fake_pio.py`.
- **Quick flash (default)**: the release now bundles prebuilt whole-chip images for all four builds in `firmware/prebuilt/` with a `manifest.json` (SHA-256, size, version, source hash). The Flash tab shows exactly what it will write and the exact esptool command, verifies the checksum first, says whether the bundle matches your source, and needs only esptool (~40 MB) — no Git, no toolchain. *Build from source* stays one click away. `tools/make_prebuilt.py` refreshes the bundle.
