Sector is ±60° azimuth, rings every 2 m to 8 m. The RD-03D only reports moving people; someone standing dead still fades out. Tracker ping speeds up and rises in pitch as the nearest person approaches.

#Dist mAngle °Speed cm/sx mmy mmGate mm

Angle: + is to the radar's left when you stand behind it looking out (the module's own convention). Speed: − approaching, + moving away. "Gate" is the module's reported range-cell size.

Experiments: sampling and burst blanker (for hunting static)
    

    

    
Averaging takes several ADC readings per sample and drops the extremes, which removes short spikes before they are heard. Changing it restarts the Stetho channel for a moment. For a record-and-compare test: choose a setting, press Record, then repeat with the other. Record in the Raw preset to hear what the ADC itself delivers.

  

  
Sound out of the node itself
    

    

    
Earphones: delta-sigma on one pin through an RC filter. I2S amp: a MAX98357A-style board. One at a time. Works with no phone connected.

  

  
Stetho channel is off. Enable it in Settings once the module and MAX4466 board are wired to the ADC pin.

  
Heart lifts 20–150 Hz (S1/S2 heart sounds), Lungs 100–1500 Hz, Voice the larynx buzz while someone talks, Wide everything, Raw the plain Doppler audio (walking people warble, about 160 Hz per m/s at 24 GHz). Knocks and bumps are ducked before they reach your ears. Use headphones near the node or you will hear feedback from the phone speaker.






  

    

  

    

  
Smart: Blip's own tracker on top of the module — stable ids, smoothing, hides one-frame flickers, wall reflections and things that wobble in place (fans, curtains). Use Show filtered on the Scope to see what it hides.

  

  

    

  

    

  

    

  

    

  

    

  
ESP32-C3 Super Mini boards have a weak antenna: at full power many phones struggle to join or drop out, so Low is their default. Raise it only if the phone is far away and connects fine.

Before you start

    
This is hobby software, work in progress, many features may be broken. It is not a medical device: the "Stetho" screen is a physics experiment, not a monitor of anyone's health. Radar can see motion through thin walls — respect other people's privacy and your local rules on 24 GHz devices.
